The model

The lever. A viewer on YouTube costs us nothing. The RTMP feed out to YouTube, X and Twitch is a flat ~113 GB for the whole day whether four people watch it or forty thousand. A viewer on our own watch page costs about $1.06 for the day.

So the watch page is the premium room — sub-second, captioned, the thing sponsors see — and YouTube is where the audience scales for free. Point the promotion at YouTube and the bill stops moving.

What the model found

Three things worth knowing before the day

01

Transcode is the biggest line we can't avoid

$45.60, and it barely moves with audience. Two egresses run: the 24-hour broadcast composite, and one recording per slot — 2,880 minutes against 600 included. The per-slot recordings are the product, so they stay. The broadcast egress only starts when there is at least one external destination enabled, which the code already checks, so a day with no RTMP destinations costs $17 less.

02

Pre-recorded segments are served from storage, to every viewer separately

A recorded episode plays as a file on the stage, and every watcher pulls it from Supabase individually. At 100 concurrent that is 371 GB across thirteen recorded slots; at 400 it is 1,485 GB. Supabase's CDN absorbs most of the cost, so the money is small — but object storage is not a video CDN, and a first-viewer cache miss on a 300 MB file is the failure mode. Worth a rehearsal with a recorded slot and real viewers before the day.

03

The intelligence is the cheapest thing in the building

Live captions for the entire 24 hours: $13.82. Claude reading all 48 segments and choosing the clips: $4.68. Together they are under 2% of the bill at 100 viewers, and they are the whole reason a podcaster would move here. The expensive part is moving pictures; the valuable part costs almost nothing.

The larger number

Working

Assumptions, and where I'd want a second opinion

  1. Viewer bitrate 1.3 Mbps for 720p WebRTC. If the stage runs 1080p this rises by roughly half, and bandwidth is already the largest variable line.
  2. Two people on stage on average, plus a producer and the captioning agent, for the whole 24 hours.
  3. Three RTMP destinations at 3.5 Mbps. Each extra destination adds about 38 GB for the day, roughly $4.50.
  4. Thirteen pre-recorded slots, matching the current booking pattern.
  5. Deepgram at the promotional streaming rate of $0.0048/min. The standard rate is $0.0077 — captions would go from $13.82 to $22.18, which changes nothing.
  6. Clip selection on Claude Opus 5. Sonnet 5 would cost $1.87 instead of $4.68 for the day. Not worth trading the quality of the pick for $2.80, but worth revisiting at a thousand shows a month.
  7. LiveKit counts a viewer's subscription as connection minutes. This is the assumption I am least certain of and it is worth confirming on the account before the day — if viewer subscriptions are metered differently, the 400-viewer scenario moves by about $216.
  8. No people costs. Nothing here prices a producer's time, and a 24-hour event needs at least two of them.
